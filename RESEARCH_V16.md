# PHASE v16 Research Notes

## Research question

What creates a durable psychological pull after the player's first local problem has been solved?

The goal of this pass was not to manufacture a larger threat. It was to create a reason for P to care about continuing through the world after Origin is repaired.

## 1. Relatedness is part of the motivational pull of games

Przybylski, Rigby, and Ryan's Self Determination Theory model of video game engagement argues that games can be compelling when they support competence, autonomy, and relatedness. Earlier empirical work by Ryan, Rigby, and Przybylski similarly found these need satisfaction variables associated with enjoyment and future play.

Design implication for PHASE:

P should not only become more capable. P should become connected to something whose state matters. The connection should be expressed through play rather than explained only through lore.

Reference:

Przybylski, A. K., Rigby, C. S., & Ryan, R. M. (2010). A Motivational Model of Video Game Engagement. Review of General Psychology, 14(2), 154 to 166. DOI: 10.1037/a0019440

Ryan, R. M., Rigby, C. S., & Przybylski, A. (2006). The Motivational Pull of Video Games: A Self Determination Theory Approach. Motivation and Emotion, 30, 344 to 360. DOI: 10.1007/s11031-006-9051-8

## 2. Do not rely on the popular version of the Zeigarnik effect

A 2025 meta analysis found little support for a universal memory advantage for unfinished tasks. However, it found a much more reliable Ovsiankina effect: people frequently resume interrupted tasks when an opportunity to continue appears.

Design implication for PHASE:

Do not merely tell the player that something remains unfinished and assume they will remember it. Give them an activity that begins successfully, is interrupted, and can later be physically resumed.

Reference:

Ghibellini, R., & Meier, B. (2025). Interruption, recall and resumption: a meta analysis of the Zeigarnik and Ovsiankina effects. Humanities and Social Sciences Communications, 12, 962.

## 3. Agency personalizes narrative memory

A 2026 Nature Communications study found that giving people control over interactive narratives changed how the story was organized in memory. Agency did not simply increase total recall. It made memories more individualized and increased the influence of personal factors on what was remembered.

Design implication for PHASE:

The important relationship should begin with something the player does. P returns to Origin and deliberately initiates the old song. The answering response therefore follows the player's action rather than arriving as a cutscene fact.

Reference:

Li, X., Ni, N. K., Born, S. J., et al. (2026). Interactive narratives reveal the personalizing effect of agency on episodic memory. Nature Communications, 17, 7272. DOI: 10.1038/s41467-026-73907-2

## 4. Effort can increase valuation, but destroying the result is not the answer

Norton, Mochon, and Ariely found that successful construction increased valuation of self made objects. Their experiments also found an important boundary condition: destroying the creation or failing to complete it weakened the effect.

Design implication for PHASE:

Do not make Origin's repair meaningless. Let the player successfully restore home and keep that victory. Move the larger unresolved problem into a relationship that remains severed.

Reference:

Norton, M. I., Mochon, D., & Ariely, D. (2012). The IKEA effect: When labor leads to love. Journal of Consumer Psychology, 22(3), 453 to 460. DOI: 10.1016/j.jcps.2011.08.002

# v16 design translation

The four Origin notes are now a call.

When the player returns to Origin and initiates the ritual, something beyond the playable field answers with a fifth note and a faint fivefold form.

The rupture scatters three home notes and severs that response.

Restoring Origin remains a real victory. Home is repaired.

But the familiar fifth response does not return from beyond the field. Instead the damaged fivefold signal nearby produces a broken echo of the same note and moves east.

The player's larger motive becomes following the broken Answer.

When Phi is later restored, the clean answering note returns and the game reveals that Phi was the voice that had always answered Origin.

That reveal does not close the entire thread. Phi is still listening farther east, preserving a larger unresolved relation beyond the local bond.

## Desired emotional structure

Create home.

Participate in a reciprocal ritual.

Lose the relation.

Repair home without erasing the larger loss.

Follow the damaged response.

Recognize the Answer in Phi.

Discover that Phi is itself connected to something farther away.
